1. How do animal brains evolve according to the notes?  
Animal brains evolve by adding new features on top of the existing parts of the brain instead of creating a new structure.

2. According to the ice-cream cone analogy by Linden, how does brain evolution occur in animals?  
For each animal brain, the entire structure isn’t remade. Instead, the old parts (the first scoops) are used and they are added on top of them to make the brain more complex than the brain’s of their ancestors.

3. What is the function of the midbrain in humans, and how does it differ from other animals?  
The midbrain in humans causes blindsight, while in frogs and reptiles, it is their main visual center.

4. Which part of the brain is responsible for movement, balance, and coordination, and what is its location?  
The cerebellum is responsible for movement, balance, and coordination, and it is located in the hindbrain.

5. Identify the function of the frontal lobe and the parietal lobe.  
The frontal lobe is responsible for decision making, motor movement, problem-solving, and movement, while the parietal lobe is responsible for sensory processing.

6. What are the functions of the thalamus and the hypothalamus?  
The thalamus acts as a relay station for sending sensory information to higher regions of the brain and relaying command signals from higher areas of the brain to different pathways. The hypothalamus is responsible for homeostasis, secretion of hormones, TALE (temperature, appetite, libido, emotion), fight or flight responses, and basic social drives.
