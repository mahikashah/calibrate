--------------------------------------------------

- small Calibrate logo/brand mark
- clear progress

Center:
- one onboarding question
- short why-we-ask explanation
- answer cards

Bottom:
- Back
- Next

Keep the content centered with generous whitespace.

Avoid overly wide forms.

2. VISUAL REFERENCE
--------------------------------------------------

Use:

public/how-it-works/step1.png

The real onboarding screen should feel recognizably similar to that preview.

Match its general ideas:

- cream/off-white background
- large question heading
- muted sage/turquoise selected state
- white answer cards
- thin dark borders

- dark charcoal text
- restrained decorative circles/shapes

--------------------------------------------------
3. PROGRESS
--------------------------------------------------

At the top of every onboarding question show clear progress.

Example:

QUESTION 1 OF 5

and a subtle progress bar or segmented line underneath it.

The progress indicator should:

- update as the user advances
- remain visually consistent
- not dominate the page
- work on mobile

The user should always understand where they are.

--------------------------------------------------
4. QUESTION PRESENTATION
--------------------------------------------------

Show ONE question at a time.

Question hierarchy should generally be:

Small progress label

Large question text

Short “why we ask this” sentence

Answer cards

Navigation

Do not place multiple onboarding questions on one page.

Do not create a long scrolling questionnaire.

--------------------------------------------------
5. WHY WE ASK THIS
--------------------------------------------------

Every real onboarding question should have a short plain-language explanation underneath it.

These explanations should help the student understand why Calibrate needs the information.

Keep each explanation to roughly one sentence.

Use wording appropriate to the actual question.

Examples of tone:

“This helps us understand what you currently rely on while studying.”

“This helps us choose a useful first technique to test.”

“This helps us understand how you usually check whether studying worked.”

“This gives us context when we compare future study sessions.”

“This helps Calibrate interpret your results more carefully.”

IMPORTANT:

Do not make unsupported psychological claims.

Do not say:

- “This reveals your learning style.”
- “This tells us how your brain learns.”
- “You are a visual/auditory/etc. learner.”

The onboarding creates a STARTING HYPOTHESIS only.

--------------------------------------------------
6. ANSWER CARDS
--------------------------------------------------

Turn answer options into polished interactive Calibrate cards.

Default state:

- white or warm off-white card
- thin charcoal/muted border
- readable dark text
- subtle hover state

Selected state:

- sage/turquoise accent
- stronger border
- visible check indicator
- clear contrast
- selected state must not rely on color alone

The selected state should resemble the answer selection shown in:

public/how-it-works/step1.png

Cards should be easy to scan and click.

On mobile, cards should become full-width and comfortably tappable.

--------------------------------------------------
7. ANSWER BEHAVIOR
--------------------------------------------------

Preserve the current intentional-selection behavior:

- clicking an answer selects it
- the selected card visibly remains selected
- selecting an answer does NOT automatically move to the next question
- the user presses Next to advance
- Back preserves previous answers
- returning to a previous question shows the previously selected answer

If the question requires an answer:

Next should remain disabled until the student selects one.

Do not silently default-select an answer.

Back

and

Next

Back
→ /how-it-works

For Question 2 and later:

Back
→ previous onboarding question

Next:

→ next question

On the FINAL question:

change the primary button text to something such as:

“See my starting hypothesis”

instead of simply “Next”

Only change this label if it does not interfere with existing submission behavior.

--------------------------------------------------
9. TRANSITIONS
--------------------------------------------------

- small slide
- smooth progress update

Avoid:

- long delays
- bouncing elements
- distracting effects

The interaction should feel responsive and fast.
